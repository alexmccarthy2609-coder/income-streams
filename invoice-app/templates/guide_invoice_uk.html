How to write an invoice in the UK

A plain-English guide for sole traders, freelancers and small limited companies: what your invoice must include, the extra rules if you're VAT registered, and how to get paid on time.

What every invoice must include

According to GOV.UK, every invoice needs:

  • A unique invoice number
  • Your business name, address and contact details
  • Your customer's name and address
  • A clear description of what you're charging for
  • The date the goods or service were provided (the supply date)
  • The date of the invoice
  • The amounts being charged
  • The VAT amount, if applicable
  • The total amount owed

Extra rules for sole traders

If you're a sole trader, your invoice must also show your own name as well as any business name you use. If you trade under a business name, include an address where legal documents can be delivered to you.

Extra rules for limited companies

Use your full company name exactly as it appears on your certificate of incorporation. You don't have to list your directors, but if you name one director on your invoices you must name them all.

If you're VAT registered

VAT-registered businesses must issue VAT invoices, which have extra requirements. A full VAT invoice normally includes:

  • Your VAT registration number
  • The time of supply (tax point), if it's different from the invoice date
  • For each item: a description, the quantity, the price excluding VAT and the VAT rate
  • The total excluding VAT, the total VAT, and the total including VAT

For sales of £250 or less (including VAT) you can usually give a simplified VAT invoice instead. The full rules are in HMRC's VAT Notice 700/21. If you charge different VAT rates on one invoice, check the rules for showing each rate separately.

Numbering your invoices

Each invoice number must be unique. Most people use a simple sequence like INV-001, INV-002… so it's easy to spot a missing one. Don't reuse a number, even if an invoice is cancelled.

Payment terms: getting paid on time

  • Put a clear due date on every invoice (14 or 30 days is common).
  • Include how to pay: your bank name, sort code and account number, or a payment link.
  • Chase politely but promptly when an invoice becomes overdue.

Late payment between businesses: if another business pays you late and your contract doesn't say otherwise, UK law generally lets you claim interest at 8% above the Bank of England base rate, plus fixed compensation of £40 (debts under £1,000), £70 (£1,000 to £9,999.99) or £100 (£10,000 or more). See GOV.UK: late commercial payments. This doesn't apply to invoices to private individuals (consumers).

Keep copies

HMRC expects you to keep records of your sales, including copies of invoices, for several years. See GOV.UK: business records if you're self-employed.

Quick checklist

  • Unique invoice number
  • Your name / business name, address and contact details
  • Client's name and address
  • Description of the work, with amounts
  • Supply date and invoice date
  • Total due and the due date
  • How to pay you
  • VAT number, rates and amounts (if VAT registered)
